## 0. Resolutions made while building the ledger

These are the places where the deck, the Friday doc or the briefs say something the files do not say in that form. The paper follows the files.

1. **"Policy sampling is 55 percent of outcome variance."** Not in the files, and the files' own figures (44.50 against 36.39, "at most about 40 percent" for difficulty) are means of per-episode sds, which cannot separate the two components. Recomputed 2026-10-05 with the one-way random-effects decomposition (`~/papers/recompute_variance.py`, pooled within-episode variance, the sampling share of the per-episode mean removed): sampling is 0.89 [0.72, 1.00] of outcome variance at the stock adversary and 0.95 [0.82, 1.00] under Wrecker, 0.72 and 0.87 without the five widest episodes (one stock episode spans sd 335 across four seeds, one Wrecker episode 966 across three). RESULTS.md's "difficulty dominates under Wrecker" does not survive the decomposition and is corrected there (dated 2026-10-05). The paper says **70 to 90 percent of per-episode outcome variance is the policy's own sampling, at both adversaries**.
2. **"The published H-MARL control was the best of six draws."** Not what the files say. The best-of-six is our own control arm `hm_w_none` (-842.76) against five null re-draws; the published figure (-132.63) is not involved. Recomputed 2026-10-05 (`means.py`): six-draw mean -883.09, sd 28.23, the control best by 40.3. The paper says **our H-MARL control is the best of six sampling draws, so every gain quoted against it is 40 points smaller than against the null centre**, and reports both. Against the centre the held O1 arm gains +455.1 and closes 0.748 of the distance to the held ceiling, beside KEEP's 0.743.
3. **"Four tables fail four disclosure axes" (ACDZero, HC-MARL).** Neither name appears in RESULTS.md or ARMS.md. The Trident artifact-identity finding and the leaderboard protocol finding are in the files (lines 2137 to 2179, 2074 to 2093). The ACDZero and HC-MARL rows came from the CAGE4 documents chat and need their own source note in the repo (which table, which paper, which axis, with the page) before the audit section is drafted. A third sourced exhibit was added on 2026-10-03 from a preprint posted this week (section 6 below), so the audit has three exhibits in hand and two to source.

4. **"+316 on H-MARL."** This is 415 minus 99 across two arms (held against dropped), not a paired per-episode delta. KEEP's +173 (79 of 100, p below 0.001) is paired. The paper says +173 paired on KEEP and quotes the H-MARL figure as a difference of arm means, or runs the paired read (`tools_paired.py` over `hm_wo_O1_g32` and `hm_wo_O1_g32_wb`) before drafting, which is one command.
5. **Reproduction figure.** The paper cites **KEEP -191.34 [-199.66, -183.02]** (four sampling seeds, published -193.68 inside the interval) and **H-MARL Expert -131.35 [-138.25, -124.44]** (published -132.63 inside). The earlier -196.06 was one sampling draw through `evaluation.py` and is mentioned only as the first reproduction.
6. **Defect count.** The overview's nine (five upstream, four local) grew to a tenth and an eleventh in the training section, and ARMS.md Known issues adds more. The canonical table in section 2 below settles it: **ten upstream code defects that shipped pipelines can hit, one that cannot (the restore-clears-training-flag defect, which goes in a footnote), four disclosure or documentation items, and twelve local-harness defects that belong in the methods section as what the instrument had to get right.** The paper's defect table has ten rows plus four disclosure rows.
7. **Upstream issues.** None of the ten is recorded as reported upstream. Items U2 (parallel seeding) and U5 (dead reward breakdown) have been open items since the first week. File U1, U2, U8 and U5 in October, before the draft cites them. U8's report should say the drop applies to every agent in the scenario, list the red durations (section 7), note that the built-in reds handle it by returning Sleep on IN_PROGRESS, and ask for an InvalidAction or a warning rather than a behavior change.
8. **"The burst loses four to one."** Stated verbally at RESULTS.md 2820 and 4373, not computed. The paper either computes it from `wo_O2_b77` against `wo_O2_g32` per decision or says "worth up to four times as much per decision" with the quote's own hedge.
9. **The 0.2 percent recovery precision.** Recomputed 2026-10-05 on all 100 episodes of `corpus_v1` (`~/papers/recompute_precision.py`): Restore 18 of 7,710 on a host with a red session, **0.23 percent** (0.19 on the first 20, which reproduces the 3 and 1,580 exactly); Remove 2,322 of 10,256, 22.6 percent. The per-step chance for a random target is 0.24 of the 95 hosts, not the "roughly 30 percent" in RESULTS.md, so Restore is about a hundredfold below chance. Pair it with the 100-episode O1 arm (-520.29, -333.0 paired, better on 1 of 100, reproduced 2026-10-05).

## 2. Canonical defect table

The paper classifies by consequence, in three tiers, with a column for where the defect lives (CybORG itself, the CAGE 4 template every submission inherits, or one submission). The agent's full extraction follows as 2b for provenance; the paper's table is 2a.

### 2a. The paper's table

## 7. Red-side exposure, checked 2026-10-05. 

SimulationController.step builds the action set in a loop over self.agent_interfaces.items(), every agent in the scenario, and the guard if self.actions_in_progress.get(agent_name) is None applies to each (lines 238 to 248). Red actions carry durations: ExploitRemoteService 4, DiscoverNetworkServices 3, PrivilegeEscalate, Impact, DegradeServices and DiscoverDeception 2 (Simulator/Actions/AbstractActions). Every built-in red agent (FiniteStateRedAgent and the Trident FSRedAgent* family) checks success.name == 'IN_PROGRESS' before choosing, so the simulator's own agents are written around the drop. A learned or LLM red that emits an action every step, as both executors in Shaikh et al. 2026 do by construction, has every action during an exploit's trailing ticks discarded with no error and no InvalidAction. Consequence for the paper: C1 is a property of CybORG for every agent, blue and red; attempt counts are not execution counts for any outside agent; the exhibits are KEEP and H-MARL on the blue side and Shaikh et al.'s per-episode action and token figures on the red side. Kill-chain reach (outcome-based) is unaffected. Not yet measured on a red trace, since no outside red agent is available to run; the claim rests on the code and the durations table, which is enough for the mechanism, and the paper says so.

**Documentation and wrapper check, 2026-10-05.** The CAGE 4 README (line 81, and `documentation/docs/pages/README.md` line 54) states: "Agent actions now have a specified time duration, which varies depending on the action chosen. Agents must wait until their action is completed before they are prompted to launch another action. Once an agent has chosen it cannot be cancelled." Its action table publishes the durations (Analyse 2, DeployDecoy 2, Remove 3, Restore 5, StealthServiceDiscovery 3, Exploit Network Services 4, PrivilegeEscalate 2, Impact 2, DegradeServices 2, DiscoverDeception 2). The tutorial `03_Actions/A_Understanding_Actions/2_Taking_an_Action.md` documents the `IN_PROGRESS` success value and `B_Blue_Actions/2_Analyse.md` gives the rationale ("a way of disadvantaging high utility actions"). The implementation departs from the README's contract: `SimulationController.step` prompts every agent on every tick and discards a submission made while the agent is busy, with no signal that distinguishes a discarded submission from a running action. The README's observation table (line 273 on) has no success index, and of the seven wrappers under `CybORG/Agents/Wrappers/` only `TrueStateWrapper.py` references the success flag, which it pops; `BlueEnterpriseWrapper`, `BlueFlatWrapper`, `BlueFixedActionWrapper` and `EnterpriseMAE` neither pass `IN_PROGRESS` through nor mask a busy agent's actions. So a submitted learned agent receives no information that its last action is still running, is asked for an action anyway, and has it discarded.

**The claim in its final form.** CAGE 4 documents that actions take one to five ticks and that an agent is not prompted while one is running. The simulator prompts every agent every tick and discards a submission made during a busy tick without any signal; the flag that reports a running action is not part of the observation the challenge wrappers expose. The two published learned defenders were trained and evaluated under a drop they could not observe, and every action or intervention count reported on this benchmark is an attempt count. The design is sound and the contract is sound; the implementation is not the contract, and nobody has counted executions. The upstream issue asks for the documented behavior (no prompt while busy, or a masked action space) or an InvalidAction on a busy-tick submission.
